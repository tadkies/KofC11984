About Our Council

Serving St. Stephen, Martyr and Holy Family Chapel, NSA Northwest in Chesapeake, Virginia since 1997.

Our History

Our council was chartered as Jesus, Mary & Joseph Council 11984 on May 25, 1997, at the military chapel at Naval Security Group Activity Northwest — the first council on a Naval installation in the Commonwealth of Virginia.

When our charter chaplain, Fr. Edward L. Richardson, passed away in April 2008, the council approved a resolution requesting a name change to Fr. Edward L. Richardson, SMA Council in memory of this beloved priest.

In November 2010 the council requested to relocate its meetings to St. Stephen, Martyr Catholic Church, effective January 1, 2011. The council continues to serve the military Catholic community at Holy Family Chapel, NSA Northwest, and the Catholic community of St. Stephen, Martyr.

Our Namesake

Fr. Edward L. Richardson, SMA (1919–2008)

Fr. Edward Richardson was born February 6, 1919 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. He earned a BA in 1943 from St. Mary’s College, did advanced studies at Villanova, Catholic University, and Notre Dame (where he earned an MA in philosophy in 1964), and later received a Doctor of Ministry degree from San Francisco Theological Seminary.

He entered the Society of African Missions (SMA) Queen of Angels seminary in 1941 and was ordained a priest on February 9, 1950. His first assignment was teaching at Immaculate Conception High School in Augusta, Georgia, where he also coached varsity basketball and baseball.

When the Korean War broke out he attended Navy Chaplain’s school in 1952 and served with the 9th Marine Regiment in Japan. A long career with the Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard followed: he rose to the rank of captain and received more than 20 medals and ribbons, including the Bronze Star, Combat Action Ribbon, Meritorious Service Medal, and Navy Commendation Medal. He named “the conversion of 642 adults to the Catholic faith” as the most significant accomplishment of his military service.

A Knight since 1939, when he joined the Order in West Philadelphia, he rose to the Fourth Degree and was appointed Virginia State Chaplain of the Knights of Columbus in 1985, and reappointed to that post regularly in the years that followed.

In 1990 he became the first Catholic priest to receive the National Brotherhood Award from the National Conference of Christians and Jews. In February 2000 he celebrated his 50th anniversary as an SMA priest.

“Build bridges with all people, never fences.”
— Fr. Richardson’s lifelong motto

After a long illness, Fr. Richardson passed away on April 26, 2008. Our council bears his name in his memory.

The Society of African Missions (SMA) is a Catholic missionary society of priests and brothers devoted to evangelization in Africa and among people of African descent.
